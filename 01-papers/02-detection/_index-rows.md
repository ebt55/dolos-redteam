## Reading order for a 48-hour project

1. **Nakamura** — pick your environment and positive control (assigned pair + private channel is a ready-made one).
2. **Bonjour** — if your project is "find the pair", this is the only method here that returns an unknown pair; implement and validate it on scripted RPS first.
3. **Rose** — if you have activations, this is the strongest detection surface, and its emergent-collusion gap is the most interesting open hole.
4. **Xu** — read before claiming any price-level or marginal-based detector works; it defines the blind spot.
5. **Rippin** — the carrier-side threat that motivates not trusting transcripts, plus a reusable coordination metric (ACI/HCI/CCI).
6. **Aharon** — the mechanism behind "same-family agents coordinate better", which is the cheapest natural control for any collusion experiment.

## Cross-cutting gaps none of these papers close

- No paper returns an unknown colluding *group* of size > 2 with a validated false-positive rate (Bonjour assumes exactly two; Rose assumes a known 4-agent roster and a probe training set).
- No paper calibrates a detector at a fixed false-positive budget on *benign* Schelling-point coordination — the false-alarm side of Q2.3 is measured only indirectly (Xu's ambient floor, Rose's null probe).
- No paper tests a detector against colluders who know they are being audited (Rose flags evaluation awareness as future work; Nakamura's agents are never told).
- No paper connects a behavioural collusion metric to a real market outcome — Xu's two halves are joined by argument, not measurement.
