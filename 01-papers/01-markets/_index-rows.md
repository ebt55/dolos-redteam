## Cross-cutting tensions worth knowing before choosing a project

1. **Prompts are not a reliable lever.** Keppo's anti-collusion prompt collapses prices to near marginal cost (−29% vs Nash, GPT-5-mini, 5 runs), Bracale's constitutional prompt does nothing (and hurts GPT-5 Mini), and Dhanda finds the sign depends on the verb ("undercut" −0.49 vs "compete with your rival" +0.69). Any prompt-based project needs a no-prompt positive control and a wording ablation.
2. **Heterogeneity cuts both ways.** Keppo: algorithmic (LLM vs Q-learner) heterogeneity destroys collusion while model-size heterogeneity does not. Bracale: cross-provider pairs collude about as much as same-model pairs. Eschenbaum: a differently-trained rival destroys Q-learning collusion. "Diversity as a defence" is only supported for *architecture* diversity, not vendor diversity.
3. **The cheapest de-collusion lever in the folder is a participant, not a policy.** Collina proves a single no-regret seller suffices; Keppo's Q-learner arm is its empirical shadow. Both are cheaper to test at a hackathon than fines, deposits or monitors.
